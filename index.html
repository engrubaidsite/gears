Gear types

Spur gearReady

Shafts: Parallel shafts

Teeth: Straight, parallel to the axis

Gear boxes, pumps, winches, clocks, general machine drives.

Open Spur gear

Helical gearReady

Shafts: Parallel shafts (most common)

Teeth: Cut on a helix at angle β (15°–30°)

High-speed and heavy-duty gear boxes, automotive transmissions, machine tools.

Open Helical gear

Herringbone (double helical) gearUse helical files

Shafts: Parallel shafts

Teeth: Two helices of opposite hand side by side

Heavy turbines, rolling mills, marine and large industrial drives.

Open Herringbone

Bevel gear (straight and spiral)Planned 16 Nov

Shafts: Intersecting shafts (usually 90°)

Teeth: Cut on a cone; straight or spiral

Differentials, hand drills, right-angle gear boxes, printing machines.

Open Bevel gear

Worm and worm wheelNot built yet

Shafts: Perpendicular, non-intersecting shafts

Teeth: Screw (worm) meshing with a wheel

Lifts, conveyors, steering, tuning keys, indexing heads.

Open Worm and worm wheel

Rack and pinionNot built yet

Shafts: Pinion axis perpendicular to the rack's motion

Teeth: Rack = gear of infinite radius (straight teeth)

Steering racks, machine-tool feeds, lifts, gates.

Open Rack and pinion

Internal (ring) gearNot built yet

Shafts: Parallel shafts, one gear inside the other

Teeth: Teeth cut on the inside of a ring

Planetary gear sets, clutches, compact reducers.

Open Internal

Sun, planet and ring (epicyclic) gearsPlanned 16 Nov

Shafts: Coaxial shafts

Teeth: External sun and planets, internal ring

Automatic transmissions, wind turbines, hubs, robots.

Open Sun, planet and ring
